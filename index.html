How a rep becomes data

Four steps, all inside the clip.

01

Sense

A 6-axis motion sensor (accelerometer + gyroscope) reads the bar more than 500 times a second.

02

Reset drift

The bar is briefly still at the top and bottom of every rep. The firmware zeroes its speed there, which keeps the numbers from drifting.

03

Sync both ends

Left and right clips share a clock over Bluetooth, so the app compares both ends of the bar at the same instant.

04

Send a summary

Only per-rep results go to your phone over Bluetooth Low Energy, not raw data. That's what lets the battery last weeks.

The engineering gotcha we planned for

On Olympic bars the sleeves spin separately from the shaft, so the clip spins with the plates. The firmware ignores rotation around the bar's long axis. Tilt and lifting motion happen on different axes, so bar speed and left-right balance stay clean.

The light ring

Lights are for the spotter.

A lifter can't see the clips mid-rep: eyes on the ceiling during bench, forward during squats. A spotter standing over the bar can, and a coach can scan a room for one red light. So the ring stays dark during normal reps and fatigue cues go to the phone.

Pairing

Pairs like AirPods.

Flip the lever, a card pops up on your phone, tap Connect. Both clips pair as one set, the way left and right earbuds do.

  • iPhone: Apple's AccessorySetupKit (iOS 18+) shows a native pairing card.
  • Android: Google Fast Pair does the same.
  • Backup: tap your phone to the NFC tag in the clip.
  • After that: clips reconnect on their own when you lock them on a bar.
  • Encrypted pairing, so nobody at the gym can grab your data.
Intent-aware

It knows a slow rep from a tired one.

Your plan tells the app what each set is for. Fatigue is only judged on the way up, so a slow, planned lowering never counts as slowing down.

Safety

Your friends are your spotters.

When a Lift Buddy's phone is near your bar, they become your spotter automatically. Two stages, so they're ready before a rep goes bad.

Get ready

Everyone fails a lift at a fairly consistent speed. When your reps trend toward that point, the light ring pulses orange and your spotter's phone taps them, before the hard rep starts.

Help now

The bar stalls for about half a second mid-rep or starts sinking. The ring flashes red, the phone plays a loud alert, and your spotter's watch buzzes.

Solo safety Beta

Pinned with no spotter for about 10 seconds? The buzzer and phone sound an alarm, then a countdown texts your emergency contact. It needs heavy testing, and we'll never market it as guaranteed safety.

For coaches

Every rack on one screen.

Each athlete's phone uploads live to the cloud, and the coach watches the whole room on a tablet. Readiness drops, imbalance spikes and missed sessions get flagged. For phone-free weight rooms, the coach's tablet pairs directly to clips on a few racks.

    The problem

    Bar-speed data is stuck in elite weight rooms.

    Velocity-based training tells a coach how hard a weight really is today. But existing trackers are one more device to strap on, pair and remember, they read only one end of the bar, and they're built for top-tier budgets. If a coach has to think about the tracker, it ends up in a drawer.

    Our answer

    Put the sensor in the clip.

    Lifters lock clips on every loaded bar anyway, so tracking happens with zero setup. Two clips read both ends of the bar. Everything runs through the athlete's phone, and every rep is sensor-verified, which makes our leaderboards impossible to fake.
